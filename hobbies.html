Hobbies

“We accept the reality of the world with which we're presented.”— The Truman Show

To make this site a little more personal, here are some films that impressed me, with a few thoughts on each. Tastes change with new experiences, so I update this page from time to time. Outside of films I enjoy classical music, playing the piano and basketball.

Movies

WALL·E

Animation · 2008

One of my favourite animated films of all time. For the first 30–40 minutes there is hardly a single line of dialogue — just a small robot with two eyes trying to do its job on Earth — yet through the movement of its eyes and body it conveys more emotion and character development than most films. The story carries a valuable message too: a planet buried in trash, a humanity that lost itself to convenience, and what gives a life meaning. It feels like the work of creative, passionate minds, and I rarely see animations like it today.

Arrival

Science fiction · 2016

An underrated film. Its cover looks like sci-fi, but it is really about communication and how it shapes our understanding of our surroundings and of time. Aliens and spaceships are only tools to tell the story of a mother. The pace is slow but never boring — the cinematography and especially the sound design are superb. And, finally, the aliens do not come to conquer Earth.

The Truman Show

Drama · 1998

Unparalleled, bizarre and paranoid. Its script might be the best I have seen: the first time I watched it I was constantly asking why, what happened, who are they — not because it is confusing, but because I was fascinated by the world it built. I have rewatched it more than any other film and always find something new, and the ending is excellent. Watch it without the trailer — and if you know how to be sure you are not living like Truman Burbank, I would love to hear it.

The Empire Strikes Back

Science fiction · 1980

Star Wars is a massive universe with some great stories and some poor ones. If I could keep only one film, it would be this one — for me the best sequel ever made. It takes the story to another level and presents the most remarkable villain in cinema through simple but powerful relationships. And John Williams' score makes it unforgettable.

The Art of Racing in the Rain

Drama · 2019

Adapted from the novel, it tells a human life from the perspective of a pure-hearted observer: a dog named Enzo. Enzo loves racing and tells the story through it — like the rain on a race track, life brings unexpected people and moments, and our self-control and discipline in facing them determine our path. Inspiring and sweet, because it has a dog!

“The best drivers focus only on the present. Never dwelling on the past, never committing to the future.”
